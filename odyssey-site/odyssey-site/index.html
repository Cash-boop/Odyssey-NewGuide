What is Odyssey?

A rite of passage, and a head start on high school.

Every 9th grader at SAAS goes. Seniors and alumni often call it one of the defining moments of their time at the school.

Get to know people

The trip is about building relationships with your classmates, your advisor, and other faculty, away from the usual classroom.

Preview the journey

You talk about what is coming up in upper school: the challenges and the opportunities, before the year gets busy.

Try something new

Cooking with your group, sleeping in a tent, and leaving your phone at the bus. A lot of it will be new, and everyone starts at the same place.

A day on the trail

Three days, start to finish.

Here is the outline from the school's advisory slides. Your advisor shares the full schedule for your group.

1

Head out

Morning departure from SAAS, about 8:15 to 8:30 AM
  • Phones go in at the bus.
  • Travel to your state park with your advisory group.
  • Eat the sack lunch you packed.
  • Set up camp, then advisory activities.
  • Dinner is cooked by students and leaders together.
2

Full camp day

Your group stays at the same park all trip
  • Advisory activities with your group.
  • Whole-group activities.
  • Meals cooked and shared together.
  • Fires only in the fire pits, and only if there is no burn ban.
3

Pack up, head home

Back on campus around 1:30 to 2:15 PM
  • Breakfast and pack up camp.
  • Lunch before you leave.
  • Ride back to campus.

Heads up: the school's slides do not list activities day by day, and times differ slightly between slides. Your advisor confirms exact times. Want a say in what the activities are? Take the survey.

Weather: pack in layers

Late September weather in western Washington can vary a lot. Plan to be outside and active in all kinds of conditions.

1 Base layer: wool or synthetic, no cotton
2 Warm layer: fleece or wool
3 Rain shell: waterproof jacket and pants

Check the forecast a few days before you go: National Weather Service, Seattle.

Leave at home

  • Cell phones and electronics (collected at the bus)
  • Speakers and gaming devices
  • Alcohol, drugs, tobacco, and vapes
  • Knives, hatchets, lighters, and weapons
  • Candy and junk food

No gear? Not a problem.

SAAS supplies group gear like tents, cooking sets, stoves, tarps, and first-aid kits. For personal gear, borrow within your group first, then ask your advisor about the SAAS Gear Library, which has a limited number of packs, bags, and pads.

Camp and food

Where you will sleep, eat, and wash up.

Your advisory group is assigned one of three Washington State Parks and stays there the whole trip.

Sleeping

Everyone sleeps in tents with their advisory group and advisor. Advisors help form tent groups. Other campers may be nearby, so respect their space.

Bathrooms and water

Each campground has restrooms and drinking-water spigots. Bring a 1-liter bottle and your camp kit.

Food

Bring a sack lunch for the first day. Every other meal is provided, and students and leaders cook together. Tell your advisor about allergies or dietary needs.

Staying safe: the campsites are remote, so help can take longer to arrive. Advisors and trip leaders have basic First Aid and CPR training. No swimming or tree climbing during the trip. Leaders cannot hand out medication, so bring your own with your personal first aid items.

Your group

You will not be on your own.

You go with your advisory group and your advisor. You camp together, cook together, and take on the trip together. These are the people who plan Odyssey and can answer your questions.

Questions

The things people wonder about.

Straight answers, based on the school's materials. If we do not know, we say so.

Do I have to go?
All 9th graders are expected to take part. Your parent or guardian also needs to finish the enrollment, medical, and permission forms before the trip. If something is making you hesitate, tell your advisor early so they can help.
What if I do not know anyone in my group?
Odyssey is partly about getting to know people, and everyone is new to the upper school. You camp with your advisory group and your advisor, and you cook and set up camp together.
What if I am nervous or get homesick?
That is normal. Talk to your advisor before or during the trip. Advisors and trip leaders have basic First Aid and CPR training, and you will have a one-on-one check-in with your advisor before the trip (in 2026 it was early September).
What if I do not have the gear?
You can borrow. Ask within your group first, then your advisor about the SAAS Gear Library, which has a limited number of packs, sleeping bags, and pads. Tell your advisor by the gear deadline so there is time to sort it out.
What happens to my phone?
Phones and other electronics are collected at the bus. Do not bring speakers or gaming devices either.
Are there bathrooms and clean water?
Yes. Each campground has restrooms and drinking-water spigots.
What about food and allergies?
Bring a sack lunch for the first day. All other meals are provided, and students and leaders cook them. Tell your advisor about any allergies or dietary needs.
What if it rains?
Late September weather in western Washington can change fast, so pack waterproof layers and check the forecast beforehand. The school's materials do not describe a rain plan, so ask your advisor or email odyssey@seattleacademy.org. This is one of the things the survey below asks about.
What will we actually do all day?
Advisory activities, whole-group activities, and cooking and eating together, according to the school's slides. Your advisor shares the details. If you want different activities, vote in the survey.
Can I swim or climb trees?
No. Swimming and tree climbing are not allowed during the trip.
